title: v2.77.2
description: Release notes for orun v2.77.2 — a lane's free slot goes to the job others wait on, a failed job no longer cancels unrelated jobs in its lane, jobs in flight no longer share a Terraform plugin cache, and sizing advice for lanes.
---

Binaries and the merged-pull-request list are on the
[GitHub releases page](https://github.com/sourceplane/orun/releases).

This release is about [lanes](../concepts/lanes.md): which job a lane runs
first, what a failure stops, and what jobs sharing a runner share. Most of it
comes from orun-cloud's first full verify on lanes: 128 jobs, packed into 16
lanes and then into 56. Plans without lanes, and lanes of one job, behave as
before.

## A lane runs the job others wait on first

A lane often holds more ready jobs than it has slots: `auto` mode can pack an
environment's whole fleet into one lane. Which ready job took a free slot was
left to chance, so a job that other jobs wait on could queue behind several
that nothing waits on.

On one measured bootstrap the API edge, the only job the console waits on,
ran last of eleven jobs in its lane at concurrency 6. It started about four
minutes into the run, and the console went live about four minutes late.

A free slot now goes to the ready job with the longest chain of jobs behind
it. The chain counts dependents anywhere in the plan, including jobs in other
lanes, so the API edge goes before workers nothing waits on. Jobs with equal
chains keep plan order. The jobs that are ready when the lane starts compete
the same way. Each job still runs as soon as its upstreams finish, and at
most `concurrency` of them at once.

## A failed job no longer cancels its lane

Every plan orun renders carries `execution.failFast: true`, and a lane read
it: the first job to fail stopped every job of its lane that had not started,
whether or not it depended on the failure. On orun-cloud, 24 of 128 jobs never
ran. A lane now stops only the jobs that depend on the failed one; the rest
run. `execution.failFast` no longer applies inside a lane.
([#735](https://github.com/sourceplane/orun/issues/735))

## Other lanes stop waiting for jobs nobody will run

A job a lane had cancelled stayed pending on the platform, with nothing failed
above it, so a job in another lane waiting on it waited out the full 60-minute
upstream limit while holding its runner. Lanes no longer cancel independent
jobs, so a job left unrun always sits below a failed upstream. A job in
another lane waiting on it sees the failure and stops at once.
([#737](https://github.com/sourceplane/orun/issues/737))

## Terraform jobs in one lane no longer corrupt each other's providers

Jobs on one runner shared one Terraform plugin cache, and `terraform init` is
not safe against a second `init` writing the same cache. Two terraform jobs in
one lane failed with `the cached package for registry.terraform.io/hashicorp/aws
… does not match any of the checksums recorded in the dependency lock file`.
Each job in flight now gets its own cache directory: a job on its own keeps
the shared one, and jobs running alongside it get siblings
(`terraform-plugins-1`, …). The directories persist, so later jobs reuse the
providers earlier ones downloaded.
([#736](https://github.com/sourceplane/orun/issues/736))

## Sizing a lane

The [lanes page](../concepts/lanes.md#size-a-lane-to-its-runner-and-its-pool)
now says how to choose `concurrency` and `maxLanes`, with the measurements
behind it. Jobs in one lane share the runner's CPUs: four at once on a 2-vCPU
runner made each job 1.8× slower, and two at once made each 1.2× slower.
